# Virtual ground is the ternary solution

```
-(0)+
```

That is the whole trick. Not three powered polarities. Two lives around a reference that is allowed to sit.

## What `(0)` is

- Flip of one cell — connected zero, inside the pair
- Hold — write of `0`, no `ds`
- VTC evaluator reference — the spine the two mirror elements are read against
- **Virtual ground** — a mid-rail the circuit *makes*, not the planet, not a third command

`LEFT / STAY / RIGHT` only exist relative to that mid.
`+1 + (-1) → 0` is the mid doing its job.

If you drive `(0)` as a third hot polarity, you deleted ternary. You built a three-state binary with extra smoke.

## What it is not

- Earth / scope clip / chassis. VTC already warns: do not bolt the probe ground to a +2.5 V virtual mid and call it science.
- A fourth gate.
- Field. Field is Dream for one `1`. Ground is the join between the two `1`s.
- A ditch between two pyramids. Zero lives inside one mirrored gate.

## Hardware reading

A virtual mid (resistive divider, buffered mid-supply, center tap) is how a bench *hosts* `-(0)+`.
Host ≠ name. If the mid sags when both sides write, the soul left and you have two singles.

Dead window around the mid = hold belt. Stay inside it: polarity 0, stamp hold. Leave it: a `1`.

## Same object

```
1(0)1   =   -(0)+   =   virtual ground   =   flip
```

Three mirrored gates = three mids that are allowed to be the same spine, read in three pairings.
One hexagon, one family of zero. Not three earths.

## Falsify

- `0` implemented as a third driven rail.
- Scope earth shorted onto the virtual mid.
- Mid collapses when `+1` and `-1` meet — then `+1 + (-1)` did not land on Ground; it landed on a sag.
